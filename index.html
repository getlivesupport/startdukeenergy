Duke Energy Start, Stop or Transfer Service: A Complete Moving Guide

Moving into a new home, leaving a rental, or changing addresses? This guide explains how to start Duke Energy service, stop electricity service, or transfer utilities without avoidable delays, duplicate billing, or a dark move-in.

Quick answer: Begin with Duke Energy’s official Start, Stop & Move service page. Have both addresses, your requested dates, unit number, identification details, and a forwarding address ready. For general service-navigation help, call (888) 620-0950.

Start, stop, or transfer: which Duke Energy request is right?

Start serviceChoose this when you are becoming responsible for a new address and need power turned on.
Stop serviceChoose this when you are leaving and no longer have responsibility for the address.
Move or transferChoose this when you are relocating and want to coordinate the old and new addresses in one request.

Do not assume that a landlord, buyer, seller, or next tenant has completed your account change. Your responsibility normally ends only when the requested stop date is accepted and processed. Keep the confirmation number, screenshot, or email until the final bill is settled.

How to start Duke Energy service at a new address

Starting Duke Energy service is the usual choice for a move-in, a new lease, a home purchase, or a location where you will be responsible for the electric account. Use phone assistance at 1(856)900-7834.

  1. Confirm the exact service address. Use the street number, street name, directional, city, state, ZIP code, apartment or unit number, and building identifier exactly as supplied by the landlord, builder, or closing documents.
  2. Select the start-service option. Use the official Duke Energy Start, Stop & Move page or your account. Choose the requested start date and review whether an appointment is needed.
  3. Complete identity and account questions. The application may request contact information and identity-verification details. Enter information only on the secure official Duke Energy website or through a verified representative.
  4. Review deposit or payment instructions. A deposit, fee, or additional verification may apply based on the account and location. Do not guess at the amount; review the live application or ask Duke Energy.
  5. Save the confirmation. Record the confirmation number, effective date, and any meter or technician instructions. Check the property on the start date and report a no-power condition promptly.
Move-in problem solver: If electricity is off after the scheduled start date, first check the main breaker and whether the address has an active meter. Never open sealed equipment or work on utility-owned equipment. Contact Duke Energy through its official channel for an urgent service-status check.

How to stop Duke Energy service when moving out

Request a stop when you leave a property, end a tenancy, sell a home, or otherwise stop being responsible for the account. A stop request is different from turning off a breaker: the account can continue billing until Duke Energy processes the request.

  1. Choose a defensible stop date. Use the date your lease, sale, or responsibility ends. If you need power for cleaning or a final walkthrough, do not choose an earlier date.
  2. Submit the stop request before moving. Provide the full address and account information if available. Ask how the final meter reading will be handled.
  3. Provide a forwarding address. This lets Duke Energy send the final bill, refund information, or other account correspondence.
  4. Document the property condition. Take a dated photo of the meter only when it is safe and accessible; do not remove covers or enter restricted areas.
  5. Confirm the final bill. Compare the final reading and dates with your records. Keep the confirmation and lease-closing documents in case the account remains open by mistake.

Important: If another person is taking over the home, do not rely on that person to stop your account. Submit your own request and let the next occupant establish service separately.

How to transfer or move Duke Energy service to another address

A transfer, often called a move request, coordinates service at a current address with service at a new address. It may be available when both locations are eligible for the same Duke Energy service. If the new address is outside the territory, you may need to stop the old account and contact the new utility instead.

  1. Gather the old address and new address, including unit numbers.
  2. Choose the date you want responsibility to end at the old location and begin at the new one.
  3. Submit the Move or Transfer request through the official portal and read every date before finalizing.
  4. Ask whether the new location needs an appointment, inspection, deposit, meter access, or adult present.
  5. Save separate confirmation details for the old and new service. Check both bills after the move.

Do not create overlapping accounts accidentally. A short overlap may be intentional when you clean or renovate, but verify the dates and charges before submitting.

What you need to start, stop, or transfer Duke Energy service

Having the right information prevents the most common application failures. Prepare:

  • Complete service address and apartment, suite, or unit number
  • Requested start date, stop date, or move date
  • Account number or recent bill for an existing account, if available
  • Government-issued identification and contact information when requested
  • Forwarding mailing address for the final bill
  • Landlord, builder, closing, or property-manager contact details if the address is new
  • Access instructions for a meter, gate, locked utility room, or appointment
  • Permit, inspection, or service-release information for new construction when applicable

Requirements can vary by Duke Energy operating company, state, property type, and account history. The live application is the controlling source for current requirements.

Common Duke Energy start, stop, and transfer problems—and how to fix them

“My address is not found”

Re-enter the address without abbreviations, then try the official USPS-style format. Check the ZIP code, directional, building number, and unit format. For a new subdivision or recently split property, ask the builder or property manager for the service address on file and contact Duke Energy if the database has not been updated.

“The application will not verify me”

Check that your name and address match your identification and account records. Avoid repeated guesses. Use the official support channel and ask what documentation is accepted; never email sensitive identity documents to an unverified number or website.

“I need same-day service”

Same-day availability is not guaranteed. Submit the request immediately, ask whether an appointment is required, and confirm whether someone must provide access. An emergency outage is different from a move-in request—use the outage reporting channel for an outage.

“I was charged after stopping service”

Compare the charge date with the confirmed stop date. A final bill, delayed meter read, prior balance, or overlapping service can explain the amount. Contact Duke Energy with your confirmation and request a written account-date review.

“The previous resident still has service”

Do not use another customer’s account or credentials. Submit your own start request with the correct move-in date and ask Duke Energy how to handle an occupied account or unauthorized-service concern.

“Power is on, but my start request is not complete”

Do not assume a working light means your account is active. Confirm the account number, service address, effective date, and billing responsibility in writing.

Duke Energy final bill, meter reading, deposit, and billing questions

After a move, billing problems usually come from a date mismatch, a missed final reading, an address error, or an account that was never formally closed. Keep:

  • the submitted request and confirmation number;
  • the start or stop date shown in the confirmation;
  • your final meter photo, if safely available;
  • lease, closing, or move-out evidence; and
  • proof of payment and correspondence.

Review the final bill for the service address, service period, meter reading, credits, deposit treatment, and forwarding address. If you dispute a charge, explain the exact date and attach only the documentation requested through a secure official channel.

Starting Duke Energy service for new construction or a remodeled property

New construction can require more than a standard move-in request. The address may not exist in the utility database, the meter may not be installed, or the local authority may need to release an inspection before energization. Ask the builder or electrical contractor:

  • Whether the meter base and service equipment are ready;
  • Whether the inspection has passed and the utility has received the release;
  • Who is responsible for temporary construction power; and
  • Whether an appointment or site access is required.

Do not energize or alter utility equipment yourself. Coordinate with the builder, local authority, and Duke Energy before scheduling a move-in start date.

Duke Energy move-in and move-out checklist

Two weeks before moving

  • Confirm which company serves the new address.
  • Submit start, stop, or transfer requests early.
  • Check whether an appointment, deposit, inspection, or meter access is needed.
  • Save confirmation numbers and requested dates.

Move week

  • Verify the old stop date and new start date.
  • Give access instructions if a technician must visit.
  • Take safe, dated meter photos and retain closing or lease records.
  • Never enter a dangerous area or remove a sealed panel.

After moving

  • Confirm the new account and service address.
  • Review both the first bill and final bill.
  • Follow up promptly if the account dates or readings are wrong.

Duke Energy start, stop, and transfer FAQs

How do I start Duke Energy service?

Use 1(856)900-7834 or Open the official Duke Energy Start, Stop & Move page, choose Start Service, enter the exact address and date, and follow the identity and payment instructions shown for your location.

How do I stop Duke Energy service when moving?

Submit a stop request through the official account or service page, select the date your responsibility ends, provide a forwarding address, and save the confirmation.

Can I transfer Duke Energy service between states?

Not every location is served by the same Duke Energy operating company. Enter both addresses in the official move tool and follow its result. If the new address is outside Duke Energy territory, contact the utility serving that address.

How far in advance should I schedule a move?

Earlier is safer; aim for about two weeks when possible. Short-notice requests may be possible, but appointments, verification, inspections, holidays, and address-database issues can delay service.
